# Glossary

Domain terms for Atlas. Use these words in code and tests.

## People

- **Campaign**: one closed game with one GM and many players. Only members can access it.
- **GM**: the one person who runs a campaign. Use "GM", not "DM". Roles belong to a campaign, not to an account.
- **Player**: the person at the table: an account that plays one or more characters in a campaign. The GM is not a player. A person without an account has no player; the GM manages their character.
- **Character**: someone a player plays in a campaign. It has a name, which starts empty and shows as "Unnamed character", and a description. Its player is the account that created it, joined with it, or claimed it. A character with no player is unclaimed. A player may have several characters in one campaign. Every member reads a character. Its player and the GM write its description. Its player and the GM write its name. Only the GM sets its status.
- **Character status**: *Active*, *Retired*, or *Dead*. A character starts active. Only the GM changes it, in any direction. Every member sees it. A retired or dead character keeps its description, notes, mentions, and player. Nothing else changes. Only the character list sets it apart: active characters first, then a collapsed section for retired and a collapsed section for dead. MCP gives the status as `status` in `list_characters` and `get_character`, and sets it with `set_character_status`.
- **Player name**: the name of the player of a character. It is the account's display name. For an unclaimed character it is the label the GM gave.
- **Unclaimed character**: a character the GM prepared for a person who has no account yet, or who never signs up. It has a player label, no account, and a claim link. Every member reads it. The GM writes its name and description. It makes nobody a member and has no player logs; player logs are optional.
- **Claim link**: the single-use link to one unclaimed character. Only the GM reads it. Whoever opens it sees the campaign and the character, signs up or signs in, and claims the character. The claimer becomes its player and a member, and the link stops working. The GM cannot claim.
- **Character description**: the rich text of a character. Every member reads it. Its player and the GM write it.
- **Secret notes**: the GM-only rich text of a character or a world entry. Only the GM reads and writes it.
- **Account**: a signed-in user. An account is not a character.
- **Member**: the GM or a player of a campaign. A player is a member as long as they have a character in it.
- **Invitation**: a reusable invite link to a campaign. It has no expiry and works until the GM revokes it. A campaign has at most one active invitation. Revoking stops future joins and keeps existing players.
- **MCP connection**: an MCP client (Claude, ChatGPT, opencode, pi) that an account allowed through OAuth. It acts as the account: it reads what the account can read, and it writes what the account can write. It deletes nothing. Its text edits are live edits, like those of an editor. It reads and edits text as blocks, by block ID, in the JSON that the editor stores (see **Block**). A bulk tool takes at most 50 items (`edit_blocks_many` also at most 500 operations). A bulk read gives a result or an error for each item. A bulk write succeeds or fails as a whole: if one item fails, nothing changes, and no editor receives an update. Every section it reads has an **Audience**, so it knows which text is secret. The account lists and revokes its connections in Settings.

## Content

- **Entry**: a named record of a campaign that a mention or a search hit points to. Kinds: character, session, note, and the six kinds of world entry.
- **World entry**: a named thing of the game world: an NPC, a location, a faction, an item, a creature, or a plot. It is free-form text. It has a name, a kind, a hidden flag, a description, and GM-only secret notes. Its kind is a label for grouping, not a schema: it adds no fields and no rules. Every detail lives in the text: state, ancestry, allegiance, history, relationships. A reader infers what the entry is from its name, its kind, and its text. Only the GM creates, edits, archives, and restores it. It is archived, never deleted. It is hidden when new.
- **World list order**: the `sort` of the World list: *Latest session mention* (the default), *Name* (A–Z), *Last edited*, or *Last created*. All but *Name* put the newest first. A tie falls back to the name. *Edited* (`edited_at`) is the last change to the name, the hidden flag, the archive state, or the description. Saving the secret notes does not move it: a player would learn when the GM wrote them. *Created* is `inserted_at`. *Latest session mention* is the highest session number whose log or player log mentions the entry (`last_session_number`); prep never counts, so the GM and a player get the same order. An entry that no log mentions comes last. Archived and hidden entries sort the same way. Each row of the list shows its edited date, created date, and latest session; the chip of the active sort stands out. The app and MCP (`list_entries`) take the same `sort` and give the same row fields: `inserted_at`, `edited_at`, `last_session_number`. The order applies only to the list without a query: a query ranks by relevance, and the sort control is off while it runs.
- **Hidden**: a world entry is hidden until the GM shows it to the players. Only the GM reads a hidden entry. The GM shows it and may hide it again. A player never sees a hidden entry: not in lists, search, mention labels (the label is "Restricted entry"), or Preview as player. Archiving does not change this.
- **NPC**: a unique individual. Not the same as a creature.
- **Creature**: a reusable monster description. The bestiary is a set of creatures.
- **Faction**: an organization. Describe its reputation toward the party in its text.
- **Note**: free-form text. Guides and handouts are notes. A plot is not a note: it is a world entry. A note has a visibility and may link to a character.
- **Skill**: a procedure that the GM writes for AI agents, for example how to write a session recap. It has a name, a description that says when to use it, and a body of rich text. A skill belongs to an account, not to a campaign: it is global and applies in every campaign. Only its owner reads or writes it, so no player and no other GM ever sees it. Only an account that is the GM of a campaign creates one. It is archived, never deleted. Its body refuses mentions, in the editor and in MCP, because a mention points into one campaign. MCP tells agents to call `list_skills` first. It lists the name and description of each skill, and `get_skill` reads the body as Markdown, for reading only: an agent follows a skill and edits it, if at all, with `read_blocks` and `edit_blocks`. Search finds a skill by its name and its text, for its owner alone, with or without a campaign filter. A skill hit has no campaign.
- **Session**: one play session of a campaign, numbered from 1. Only the GM manages it.
- **Session status**: *In prep* or *Done*. A session starts in prep. The GM marks it done after play and may move it back. Every member sees the status.
- **Log**: the final record of a session. Members read it. Only the GM edits it.
- **Conflict**: two logs of one session that directly contradict each other about the same fact. A log that leaves out a detail is not a conflict. The AI draft of a log reports conflicts to the GM only, never in the draft text.
- **Prep**: the GM-only text of a session. The GM plans in it and takes live notes in it during play.
- **Player log**: one player's recollection of a session. One per player per session. Every member reads every player log. Its author and the GM write it. The GM is not a player and writes none of their own.
- **Progress/rewards**: one campaign text that records what already happened. Members read it. Only the GM edits it.

## Text

- **Section**: one rich-text field of a resource, for example a campaign description. A section has its own access rule and its own revision number.
- **Live editing**: everyone who may edit a section edits it together and sees changes at once. The same editor does this for every section. The server keeps the shared document and checks the user's rights again on every message. Anyone who may read a section but not edit it sees the changes live and cannot type.
- **Presence**: who has a section open. Everyone who may read the section sees it, readers included. The server sets each person's name and colour. A client can send only its cursor.
- **Saved**: the server committed the change to the database. The editor shows "Saved" only after the server confirms it.
- **Revision**: the counter of a section's JSON. The server raises it each time live editing stores a change. No API action saves a section's JSON.
- **Epoch**: the number of times the shared document was rebuilt from the section's JSON, because something other than live editing changed the JSON. An editor that holds an older epoch must start over.
- **History**: the saved versions of a section. A restore creates a new revision.
- **Checkpoint**: a user-visible history version, made after 30 s without edits or every 5 min of editing.
- **Mention**: an inline @ link to another entry. It stores the kind and the ID, and shows the current name. Kinds: character, session, note, NPC, location, faction, item, creature, plot. A player is not a kind: mention the character. Every rich-text section of a campaign accepts mentions; the body of a skill does not. The author may mention any entry the author can read, hidden ones included. A reader who cannot read the entry sees "Restricted entry". MCP tools read a mention as a node with the kind, the ID and a read-only `label`, the current name looked up with the access of the reader; a write ignores the label. Search and the AI log draft read it as the plain name. An archived note or world entry keeps its name in a mention, and the picker offers it with an "Archived" tag.
- **Block**: one unit of a section, as the editor stores it: an `id`, a `type`, `props`, `content` and nested `children`. Atlas allows only the types in `Atlas.RichText.Document`. MCP clients read and edit text by block ID, with `edit_blocks`; an update can carry the block's `hash` to refuse the edit when the block changed since the client read it. An MCP edit that removes an image needs `remove_images`, and one that deletes more than 10 blocks needs `delete_many`; an update that drops bold, links or mentions, or that has no `hash`, still applies and returns a warning. The text of an MCP edit is never Markdown.
- **Search**: shared fuzzy search for names and text the account may read, used by the app API, MCP, and AI. Campaign is an optional filter; without it, search covers all campaigns the account may read. Matching ignores case, accents, and punctuation, and accepts one edit for query words of 4–7 characters or two for longer words. A query word is a run of letters and digits that punctuation joins without a space; the query M.A.77 asks for ma77, which matches the text M.A.77 and MA77, never the letters m and a alone. Every query word must match, in any order, in the entry name together with one section. Titles rank before text: an exact title match first, a title match with a typo second, then exact words in the text, then near words. Within a rank, a title that is the query comes first, then one that starts with it, then the one with more matches in the text. The app and MCP show hits in this order, not grouped by kind. Hits include campaign names and IDs, with block IDs and plain-text snippets. Hidden entries and sections never affect matches, ranking, snippets, or counts. Archived notes and archived world entries are found like the others; the hit has `archived: true` and the app marks it. Archived skills are excluded. Search takes an optional `archived` filter, true for only archived notes and world entries and false for only active ones, and an `offset` that skips hits of the ranked list to read the next page; `more` says whether hits remain after them. The quick access menu, the World list, and MCP use this one search. The World list shows 25 entries per page. Without a query it pages the full list. With a query it asks Search for one page, with the kind and archived filters of the list.
- **Image**: an uploaded picture, converted to WebP. It stands alone, with no parent. A document stores its ID, never a URL.

## Access

- **Shared**: readable by all members. Editable by all members once the note has visibility Everyone.
- **GM-only**: readable and editable only by the GM.
- **Visibility**: who reads a note. The owner or the GM sets it. Levels: Private, Owner and GM, Character, Everyone. A new note starts at Owner and GM.
- **Private**: only the owner reads the note. The GM does not read a player's private note.
- **Owner and GM**: the owner and the GM read the note.
- **Character note**: a note with visibility Character. The owner, the GM, and the player of the linked character read it. That player also edits the body. A player links only their own character. Until a player claims the character, only the owner and the GM read it. After the claim, the player reads it. Only the owner and the GM rename, share, or archive it.
- **Everyone**: all members read the note and edit its body.
- **Owner**: the member who created an entry. The owner manages it. The GM also manages it, except a player's private note.
- **Audience**: who reads one section. Values: GM only, Private, Owner and GM, Character, Everyone. The last four are the note visibility levels; GM only is the fifth. The app shows it as a badge next to the section title. MCP gives it as `<section>_audience` in every record, and as `audience` in `read_blocks`, `edit_blocks` and search hits. A section has one audience: secret notes and prep are GM only; a skill is Private; a note body has the visibility of the note; the description of a hidden world entry is GM only until the GM shows it; all other sections are Everyone. The MCP server instructions tell an agent never to copy, quote, summarize, or hint at text for a wider audience than its own, unless the GM asks. `edit_blocks` warns when text that players read mentions a hidden world entry. The tool descriptions of `set_entry_hidden` and `set_note_visibility` warn that they publish text.
- **Archived**: removed from the default lists, never deleted. The owner or the GM can restore it. An archived note or world entry keeps its readers: whoever read it before still opens it, mentions it, and finds it in search. Hidden and visibility still decide who reads it. A player cannot edit an archived note. A list shows archived entries only when asked.
- **Restricted entry**: the label a reader sees for a link to an entry they cannot access, in a mention.
- **Preview as player**: a read-only page that shows a campaign the way a player sees it.
